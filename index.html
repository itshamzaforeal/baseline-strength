The method

Training programmed from your numbers, not a template.

Most programs online are written for an average lifter who doesn't exist. They assume a starting squat depth, a hip mobility, a bar path — and hand everyone the same sets and reps regardless of whether their technique can support the load yet. That's how people get strong on paper and get hurt in practice.

We start differently. Every client's first session is a movement assessment: we film your squat, hinge, and press from multiple angles, check joint-by-joint range of motion, and establish your baseline numbers — the working weights and technique markers you can currently repeat with good form, not your one-off max. That baseline is the zero point everything else gets measured against.

From there, programming is a series of small, deliberate increases against a plan built for your baseline specifically — not a percentage chart lifted from someone else's program. We retest on a fixed schedule, log every session in your training file, and adjust the plan to what the numbers actually show, not to how the week felt.

How it works

Four steps, in this order, every time.

The sequence matters — programming before assessment is exactly the shortcut we're built to avoid.

01

Movement assessment

A 75-minute session where we film and measure your squat, hinge, and press, check mobility at the joints that limit them, and flag any technique faults before load is added.

02

Baseline numbers established

We convert the assessment into working numbers: the loads, ranges, and cues you can currently repeat correctly. This is your zero point, logged in your training file.

03

Programmed training block

A block of 4–6 weeks written directly from your baseline, with weekly form-check video review so technique holds as load increases.

04

Retest & progress

We re-run the baseline lifts, compare against the last block's numbers, and write the next block from what actually changed — not from how it felt.
